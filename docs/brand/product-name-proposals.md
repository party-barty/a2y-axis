# Product Name Proposals — The AI Pet Translator

**Date:** 2026-05-08
**Status:** ✅ **LOCKED 2026-05-08 — the product is named Petsky.** Alternates retained below for reference / future version-bump scenarios.

The recurring fictional case-study product is the AI pet translator app — a wearable + app that "translates" pet barks/meows into English. Vaporware-pitch energy, comedy not harm. The name should sound like a real consumer AI product launched in the last 18 months while leaving room for the joke that it doesn't quite work.

## Constraints

- Reads as a current-cycle Series B AI startup product — not enterprise, not childish
- Audience filter: AI-semi-pro PM-on-web crowd who follows OpenClaw discourse
- Tonal exclusions: no femme themes; no masculine stereotypes (sports, nautical, military, frat)
- 1–3 words, app-store-friendly
- Pronounceable on the first try
- Ideally evokes the pet space without being exclusively dog-coded or cat-coded

## Candidates

### 🏆 LOCKED: **Petsky**

The most generic, plausible Series B AI startup name in the lineup. Sounds like every YC company at demo day. Carries the *"we raised $18M from Andreessen and Greylock"* energy without promising anything specific — which is exactly the joke. The mid-ness *is* the comedy. Ships easy across all surfaces (homepage hero, app store, podcast ad reads, Slack bio). Pet-coded, not species-specific.

**Rating:** 🔵 Wins on plausibility, mid-AI-startup-name vibe, joke-readiness.

### Yapp

Yap (dog onomatopoeia) + app. Maximally on-the-nose. App-store-friendly, single syllable, easy to repeat. Slightly more dog-coded than cat — fine, but worth flagging. Risk: too cute / too pun-forward; might tip the comedy from dry-vaporware to overt-jokey.

### Translatail

Translate + tail. The most direct comedy name on the list. Sounds like a children's-app pun. Strong if you want the joke loud; weak if you want plausibility. Dog-coded.

### Mewsly

Meow + muse + early-2010s-startup-name pattern (Bitly, Spotify, Bumble). Almost-too-cat-coded but not quite. Has a faint *"we did the seed round on a vibe"* energy.

### Pawser

Paws + pause + parser. Riffs on "the AI listens, then translates." Decent register; slightly mid. App-store-fine.

### Bark Forth

Two-word, slightly absurd, strong Office-corporate-naming energy ("Sabre" + "Bark"). Dog-coded. Can imagine it as the name of a slide in a TechCrunch teardown.

### Snoots

Lowercase, friendly, vague enough to cover dogs and cats. Has the contemporary AI-startup whiff — single word, slightly weird, reads as confident. Slight risk of being *too* charming and softening the vaporware joke.

### Wagner

Like the composer + wag. Series B startup that takes itself extremely seriously. Pure absurdity once you say it out loud. Dog-coded but the high-art reference helps.

### Petfound

Riffs on *"profound."* The AI's insights are deeply mundane; the name suggests otherwise. App-store-friendly.

### Lingua

Generic enough to feel like a placeholder. Could work if paired with a sub-product (*"Lingua Pet"*) but on its own it's bland.

## ✅ Locked: **Petsky** (2026-05-08)

It's the most plausibly-real of the candidates and the most generic. That generic-ness is the comedy: the user encountering "Petsky" doesn't immediately think "this is a joke" — they think "huh, another pet-AI startup" — and *that* is exactly the moment the studio voice lives in. The vaporware energy works best when the wrapper is fully convincing.

Held in reserve as alternates if the comedy register ever needs to shift: **Yapp** (louder), **Snoots** (quieter).
